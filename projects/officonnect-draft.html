OffiConnect

OffiConnect is a scheduling and room-booking app that helps UC Davis Transportation Services run a hybrid office with too few rooms.

Summary

I designed OffiConnect end to end — survey, stakeholder interview, flows, prototypes and final UI — so staff can see their day, book shared rooms and create meetings in a few taps.

Role

Solo designer
UX research
Interaction & visual design

Partner

UC Davis Transportation Services
Stakeholder: Lucas Griffith, Campus Planning and Environmental Stewardship

Timeline

09/2022 – 03/2023

Original page says “10 weeks” — confirm the real duration.

Prompt

COVID-19 made hybrid meetings the norm at UC Davis Transportation Services — but the office didn't change with it. A growing team was sharing a fixed number of rooms and desks, and the existing meeting system was confusing to use.

The result: double-booked rooms, field staff missing meetings, and volunteers without a desk to sit at. The department needed a new, flexible office model and a tool to run it.

Research

I surveyed 23 employees on how long they spend in meetings, how they meet (in person, online, hybrid) and which app features they would value most.

Competitive review

Flexible-workspace providers already solve room and desk booking at scale, so I studied how Industrious, WeWork and Regus handle it — looking for user frustrations to avoid, usability patterns to borrow, and gaps in features.

Constraints

Fixed space. No new rooms were coming — the design had to make better use of the existing ones, including furniture and shared desks.

Mixed workforce. Office staff, field employees and volunteers all needed the same tool, but with very different schedules and needs.

Solo project. I owned research, design and testing alone, so I kept scope to the three jobs the research pointed to: see my day, book a room, set up a meeting.

Shaping

I mapped the core flow to make booking as short as possible:

  • A conference room can be reserved in two steps.
  • Meetings and personal tasks are created from the same screen.
  • Staff are grouped by department, so organisers can invite a whole team at once.

Solution

See my day

A sliding calendar and card-based task list. Icons and colour replace text labels so the day can be scanned at a glance.

Book a room in two steps

Pick a room or piece of furniture, pick a time. Everyone invited is notified, and no two people can book the same desk at once.

Create a meeting

Switch between a personal task and a meeting on one screen; invite whole department groups, each with its own icon.

Outcome

I presented the app on site to Lucas Griffith and the team, showing how it would run their office. The design was handed over to UC Davis Transportation Services as part of the upgrade being considered for their building, and the department planned an office renovation for the following year.

Add any result you have: did staff test the prototype? How many steps/minutes did booking take before vs after? Was any part adopted? One number here makes the whole case study stronger.

Reflection

Start from the space, not the screen. The real problem was a shortage of rooms and desks; the app only works because it treats them as shared resources.

Shorter is better. Every iteration removed a step or a screen. Simplifying the flow mattered more than adding features.

Follow through. I'm continuing to follow the department's renovation plan to see how the design is used.
